The Zero Waste Cities Challenge was a specialized circular economy startup accelerator launched by the Massive Earth Foundation (MEF) in partnership with WasteAid's Circular Economy Network.

The program was designed to tackle urban waste management crises by incubating early-stage companies converting waste into resource loops.

## Global Scope

MEF acted as the core Business Incubation Support Partner for the challenge. The initiative ran simultaneously across three major urban clusters facing severe garbage infrastructure bottlenecks:

- Guwahati, India
- Johannesburg, South Africa
- Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam

## Funding & Support Structure

- **Financial Reward:** The top circular innovations from the cohorts competed to win a €10,000 cash prize to scale up their operations.
- **Bootcamp Focus:** Selected founders went through a 3-month virtual intensive track covering pitch deck creation, financial forecasting, and cash flow modelling to make them institutional-investment ready.
- **Design & Communication:** Startups received specialized workshops (e.g., partnering with premier design firms like Treemouse) centered on product usability and seamless consumer communication.

## Selected India Startups & Innovations (Guwahati)

During its implementation in Guwahati, the challenge scouted and backed 12 prominent startups driving localized waste-loop alterations:

- **recycleX:** Building sustainable construction materials utilizing industrial and plastic waste.
- **Golden Feather:** Pioneering the recycling of chicken feather waste into handloom fabrics and paper.
- **PampEarth:** Creating eco-friendly, biodegradable alternatives to combat sanitary and diaper waste.
- **The Midway Journey:** A grassroots circular hub driving community plastic collection and zero-waste public awareness.
- **Care North East Foundation & Shree Guru Plastics:** Scaling infrastructure for organized plastic collection and mechanical recycling in northeast India.

## The Broader Context: 20 Cities Towards Zero Waste

Beyond MEF's startup challenge, the term "Zero Waste Cities" aligns with a massive global movement. The UN Secretary-General's Advisory Board on Zero Waste (supported by UNEP and UN-Habitat) launched the 20 Cities Towards Zero Waste initiative.

The program highlights 20 global urban centers turning ambition into local law through food waste prevention, informal picker inclusion, and strict producer responsibility. Notably, Varkala (Kerala) was selected as the representative city from India.
